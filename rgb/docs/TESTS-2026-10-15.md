# Tests of 2026-10-15: LED rotation, ring routing, MCU on the front

Everything here is a first look, done with scripts on the placement sketch. What is **measured** (a script ran and produced the number) is separated from what is only an **estimate** (a model).

## 1. LEDs turned radially (done)
Each of the 132 ring LEDs is now at its own radial angle, as on the single-colour ring: KiCad rotation = 90 degrees minus the clock angle, so the LED's local +x points outward (`tools/build_sketch.py`, option `--led-offset` adds 0/90/180/270 on top). With offset 0 the pads are: pad 1 (common anode) outer / counter-clockwise, pad 2 (red) outer / clockwise, pad 3 (green) inner / clockwise, pad 4 (blue) inner / counter-clockwise. Positions are the old board's angles, so minute and second LEDs sit at 6 degree steps (non-90 degree rotations, like the single-colour board; the JLCPCB placement preview has to be checked for this, as before).

**Measured gaps** (LED 1.0 mm across, rings 15 / 17.5 / 20 mm): tangential gap between neighbouring LEDs 0.83 mm on the minute ring and 1.09 mm on the second ring (3 and 4 tracks of 0.1 mm at 0.1 mm clearance fit), radial gap between rings 1.5 mm (7 concentric tracks), hour ring 6.9 mm.

## 2. Routing the ring with the grid router (`tools/ring_route_test.sh`)
All 40 ring nets (22 row lines, 18 colour lines R1-R6, G1-G6, B1-B6, each colour line visits 22 LEDs spread over the whole watch) routed among the LED pads only, colour lines first, tool `single-color/tools/grid_route2.py` (3 signal layers F / In1 / B, 0.1 mm tracks and clearance, 0.15 mm vias). One colour line alone routes in about a minute.

| case | row lines routed | colour lines routed | time |
|---|---|---|---|
| rings 15/17.5/20, LED offset 0 | 22 of 22 | **0 of 18** | 28 min |
| same, offset 90 | 22 of 22 | 2 of 18 | 24 min |
| same, offset 180 | 22 of 22 | 1 of 18 | 32 min |
| same, offset 270 | 22 of 22 | 1 of 18 | 32 min |
| same, offset 0, 4 signal layers | 22 of 22 | 0 of 18 | 35 min |
| rings 16.5/19/21.5 (board R 23.5), offset 90 | 21 of 22 | 1 of 18 | 14 min |
| rings 17.5/20/22.5 (board R 24.5), offset 90 | 20 of 22 | 0 of 18 | 18 min |

**Reading it:** the router cannot connect the colour lines, whatever the LED turn, a fourth layer or 1.5-2.5 mm bigger rings. I first guessed "the rings are too tight" and the larger-ring runs **did not support that guess**. A connectivity check of the offset-90 result shows what happens: the nets routed first take the space (one colour line ended up with 367 track pieces in four separate groups), and later colour lines are starved (the B2 line has 20 of its 22 pads still isolated, with 12 tracks in total). That is the weakness of a greedy router on nets that go all the way round: it does not plan lanes. So this test says "this router is the wrong tool", **not** "the ring is impossible"; the old RGB board proves a ring at 14 / 16.5 / 19 mm routes (6,002 track pieces, 572 vias) when the lanes are planned. The test did not use the ground plane (In2), which the old board's routing also used for signals in places.

## 3. How big does the ring need to be? (estimate only)
The single-colour project has a capacity model for ring routing with long nets as concentric lanes (`single-color/tools/polar_model.py`, first-principles, "to be confirmed by geometry and DRC"). With our LED (1.0 mm, pads 0.4 at 0.6), 18 long nets, lane pitch 0.345 mm, it gives:

| front arrangement | free radius needed inside the rings | rings (hour/minute/second) | board diameter |
|---|---|---|---|
| cell + buttons + IMU + mode lights only (MCU and row switches on the back) | 12.8 mm | 17.2 / 19.3 / 21.4 | **45.0 mm** |
| DB48 MCU on the front (variant B) | 12.9 | 17.3 / 19.4 / 21.5 | 45.2 mm |
| DB64 MCU on the front, cell shifted 2 mm (variant A) | 14.1 | 18.5 / 20.6 / 22.7 | 47.5 mm |
| the old routed board (free radius about 12.5), for reference | 12.5 | 16.9 / 19.0 / 21.1 | 44.4 mm (the real board is 43.0 with its 0.83 mm LEDs) |

The model reproduces the old board's size within about 1 mm, which is why I take it seriously, but it is a model. **If it holds, the sketch's rings (15 / 17.5 / 20) and 44 mm are about 1-2 mm too small** and the 24 row switches should not be on the front (they cost the 14.1 vs 12.8 mm difference, about 2.5 mm of board diameter). I have **not** changed the main sketch for this; it needs a real lane routing to confirm.

## 4. MCU on the front
`tools/front_mcu_study.py` packs the front items into the free disc (simulated annealing, so each number is approximate, shorter runs are noisier). Smallest free radius F (inner ring radius = F + 0.9):

| | F |
|---|---|
| AVR128DB64 (10.3 x 10.3 mm courtyard) + 7 decoupling caps + crystal and 2 load caps, cell shifted 2 mm | **14.06** |
| the same with the cell centred | 16.3 |
| AVR128DB48 (7.7 x 7.3 mm) + 6 caps + crystal, cell centred | **12.89** |
| nothing but the cell, buttons, IMU, mode lights (no MCU) | 12.81 (the cell's own corners: 12.78) |

So the **solar cell** is what sets the free radius; a DB48 on the front costs essentially nothing, a DB64 costs about 1.2 mm of free radius and forces the cell off-centre. Two variant boards were built and pass the courtyard/ring checks: `variants/front-mcu-db48.kicad_pcb` (rings 14.5/17/19.5, R 21.8; the row switches are driven by three 74HC595 shift registers on the back; 30 of the DB48's 38 I/O used) and `variants/front-mcu-db64.kicad_pcb` (rings 15/17.5/20, R 22, cell 2 mm down). The DB48 variant needs three 74HC595 (e.g. 74HC595BQ C730243, 147 k in stock, or the basic SOIC-16 C5947) and a firmware change (rows through the SPI peripheral). Neither variant has the fan-out room around the MCU checked.
