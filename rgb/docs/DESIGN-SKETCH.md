# RGB watch: first sketch (2026-10-15)

**What this is:** a placement study, not a design yet. It answers "can the single-colour watch's feature set (solar harvester, two LiPo cells, buzzer, accelerometer, two buttons, UPDI pads) be combined with 132 RGB LEDs that JLCPCB can assemble, and how big does the board have to be?" There is no schematic and nothing is routed. The picture files are `../renders/top.png` and `../renders/bottom.png`; the board is `../LED Watch - RGB.kicad_pcb`, generated by `../tools/build_sketch.py`. The previous RGB board (Harvatek LEDs, ATmega165PA) is archived in `../archive/2026-10-14 old RGB board ...`.

## The answer in numbers

| | single-colour (rev A) | old RGB board (archived) | this sketch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board | 42.1 mm circle + lugs, 51.3 mm tall | 43.0 x 50.4 mm | **44.0 mm circle + lugs, 51.1 mm tall** (radius 22.0) |
| LED rings (hour / minute / second) | 14.6 / 17.4 / 19.6 mm | 14.0 / 16.5 / 19.0 mm | **15.0 / 17.5 / 20.0 mm** |
| LEDs | 132 white + 7 mode | 132 RGB | **132 RGB + 7 RGB mode lights** |
| MCU | AVR128DB48 (48 pins) | ATmega165PA (not stocked) | **AVR128DB64 (64 pins, 52 usable I/O)** |

Everything fits at radius 22 mm with every part inside the board (0.6 mm to the edge), nothing in the solar-cell keep-out on the front, nothing in the two battery keep-outs on the back. At radius 21.5 mm only the buzzer is 0.3 mm too far out, so 22 mm is close to the minimum for this arrangement; a different arrangement of the back might save a little.

## What goes where

**Front** (face): the three LED rings; the solar cell pads at the left and right of the centre (the 23 x 8 mm cell covers the centre band, nothing may stand there); the seven mode lights in a row just under the cell; the accelerometer and the two buttons below that; and above the cell, the 24 row switches (see below).

**Back:** the two LiPo cells keep their places from the single-colour board (top and bottom, 16.5 x 16 mm each, centred); between them the BQ25570 harvester block, copied from the single-colour board; on one side the buzzer and the two battery plugs; on the other side the **MCU**, the crystal and its capacitors, the 18 column resistors, decoupling and the UPDI pogo pads.

**Why the MCU is on the back:** the 9 x 9 mm QFN-64 does not fit on the front. With the solar cell band across the middle, the front would need an inner free radius of about 15 mm for it (it has to sit between the cell keep-out and the ring); the inner ring is at 15.0 mm, which leaves about 14.1 mm. The back has the room.

**Moved to the back (your request):** MCU, crystal, column resistors, decoupling capacitors, I2C pull-ups, plugs, buzzer, harvester. Only parts that must see the front (LEDs, cell pads, buttons, accelerometer) or that fit nowhere else (the 24 switches) are on the front.

## The LED

JLCPCB has two usable 0.8 x 0.8 mm RGB LEDs, **both common anode** (the anode is shared, each colour has its own cathode). The old Harvatek part is common cathode and not stocked; nothing common-cathode at that size is in stock (see `../archive/2026-10-14 old RGB board - routed, Harvatek LEDs, ATmega165PA/production/fab-rfq 2026-10-05/jlc-alternatives.md`).

| | Nationstar NCD0808RGB1 (C2917192) | Meihua MHPA0808RGBDT (C404279) |
|---|---|---|
| Stock / price | 6,179 / $0.060 | 13,228 / $0.047 |
| Forward voltage (the important number) | typical R 1.9, G 2.5, B 2.6 V; max R 2.2, G 3.2, B 3.2 V (at 2 mA) | R 1.7-2.3 V, **G and B 2.5-3.5 V** (at 5 / 3 mA), no typical, no bins |
| Max current | 10 mA | 20 mA |
| Pin layout | blue top-left, + top-right, green bottom-left, red bottom-right | **identical** |

**Recommendation: Nationstar NCD0808RGB1** because its green and blue forward voltage is lower and has a stated typical and maximum. The footprint I made (`../footprints/LEDWatchRGB.pretty/RGB0808_CommonAnode`, pads 0.4 mm at +-0.3 mm) accepts either part, so the choice can be changed at order time.

**The warning that matters:** green and blue LEDs need about 2.5-3.2 V and the battery is 3.0-4.2 V. Driven through a plain resistor, brightness changes a lot with battery voltage and from LED to LED. My arithmetic (3 mA aim at 3.7 V: 470 ohm for red, 270 ohm for green and blue, 0.3 V lost in the MCU pin; an estimate, not measured):

| battery | red typ / worst | green typ / worst | blue typ / worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.3 V | 2.3 / 1.7 mA | 1.8 / 0 mA | 1.4 / 0 mA |
| 3.7 V | 3.1 / 2.5 mA | 3.3 / 0.7 mA | 2.9 / 0.7 mA |
| 4.1 V | 4.0 / 3.4 mA | 4.8 / 2.2 mA | 4.4 / 2.2 mA |

("typ" = an LED with the typical forward voltage, "worst" = one with the maximum.) So white will drift towards red as the battery drops, and an LED at the top of the voltage range can be dim. Fixes, in order of effort: (1) buy a few LEDs and measure real ones before deciding; (2) firmware compensation by battery voltage (raise the on-time as the battery falls); (3) a 5 V boost rail for the LEDs, which also needs level shifting for the row switches; (4) constant-current column drivers. I would do (1) first.

## How the 132 LEDs are driven

Same idea as before: a matrix. **Rows** (24): the seconds ring has 10 rows of 6 LEDs, the minute ring 10, the hour ring 2, plus 2 for the 7 mode lights. **Columns** (18): R1-R6, G1-G6, B1-B6, the position of the LED within its row. Because the LEDs are common-anode the row line is the anode: each row gets a small P-channel switch to the battery (24 of them, on the front above the cell), and each column line goes through a resistor into an MCU pin that pulls it low to light that colour. One row is on at a time; its up to 6 LEDs x 3 colours can draw about 50-90 mA, which a pin could not carry but the switch can. Each column pin only ever carries one LED colour, about 3 mA.

- **Row switch: Diodes DMP21D0UFB4-7B** (C155329): -20 V, 0.495 ohm maximum at 4.5 V gate drive, -0.55 A, gate-protected, a 1.0 x 0.6 mm package. Datasheet read; the **pin order (which pad is gate and which source) I could not read out of the datasheet text**, so it must be confirmed on the drawing before the schematic. A basic-library fallback, AO3401A (C15127, SOT-23), has a known pin order but is too big for 24 on the front.
- The MCU runs from the same node as the LEDs, so a high pin is exactly the switch's source voltage and the switch is fully off; floating pins at power-up light nothing because the column pins float too.

**Pin plan** (`pin-plan.csv`): rows on ports D, C and G (full ports, good for fast writes), columns on E, A and B, I2C on PA2/PA3, buzzer on PB4/PB5 as before, buttons, accelerometer interrupt and BAT_OK on PF2-PF5, crystal on PF0/PF1, **2 spare pins** (PB6, PB7). Total 50 of the 52 usable I/O.

**LED to position map:** `../data/led_matrix.csv`, taken from the old routed RGB board (which LED is which second/minute/hour, which row, which column).

## Re-use of the old routed ring

The old RGB board is fully routed (6,002 track pieces, 572 vias, DRC 0 errors, 0 unconnected), so rings at about 14 / 16.5 / 19 mm with four-pad LEDs are proven routable. I kept that ring geometry (stretched by 1 mm for the extra edge room and easier routing) and kept every LED in the same position and rotation. The new LED has to be turned by 90 degrees, and I **checked by calculation** that with that turn all 132 x 4 pads land in the same corner as the corresponding net on the old board (`../tools/check_pad_corners.py`: 0 differences). That means the old row/column assignment carries over without change, and the old ring copper could be re-used after scaling.

One thing I checked on the way: the old board's LED footprint numbers its pads differently from the Harvatek datasheet, but the colours sit in the right physical corners for the Harvatek part turned by 90 degrees (common top-left, red top-right, green bottom-right, blue bottom-left), so the old board was wired consistently. The same turn puts the new LEDs' pins on the same nets.

## Open questions and risks (ranked)

1. **LED brightness spread** (above). Needs a real sample.
2. **MCU stock:** 11 AVR128DB64-I/MR plus 5 of the -E/MR variant at JLCPCB. Fine for 5 boards, not for a second batch without a restock. The TQFP version (AVR64DB64-I/PT, 77 in stock) has a different footprint.
3. **DMP21D0UFB4 pin order** to confirm; then the 24 gate/source/drain connections follow.
4. **Board size** 44 mm (vs 42-43): your call. The back dictates it, not the face.
5. **LED polarity in the JLCPCB viewer:** with common-anode LEDs, check the "+" marker on the placement preview as we did before.
6. **Mode lights as RGB** (7 more LEDs and 2 more rows) vs. single-colour white: I chose RGB because it is one part type and needs only 2 extra pins; easy to change.
7. **Gate pull-ups** (24 resistors) are not in the sketch; I argued they are not needed because floating pins also leave the columns open, but it is worth one sentence from an engineer.
8. The BQ25570 side (cell, charging, rails) is unchanged from the single-colour board and has the same untested status.
