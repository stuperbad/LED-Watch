## 3. Watch load (3 LEDs only, dimmed, shown only when woken)
Assumptions (ESTIMATES unless marked): 3.7 V rail; 3 LEDs on for 3 s; MCU active 1.5 mA while the display is up; MCU deep sleep + RTC 3.3 uA (derived from the AVR128DB datasheet table); 3.3 V regulator quiescent 1.6 uA (MCP1700, datasheet); self-discharge 2%/month of a 100 mAh (370 mWh) cell; BMI270 wake-on-motion ~13 uA @ 3.3 V and BMA400 low-power ~0.85 uA @ 3.3 V (both VERIFIED by the research agent).

A 100 mAh cell (370 mWh) would last about 330 / 310 / 170 days at those three, with NO solar. (The earlier research assumed 10 LEDs at 2 mA, ~10x more display energy.)

## 4. PWM and multiplexing are the same trick
Multiplexing already switches each LED on for only part of the time (3 lit LEDs in different rows -> each on 1/3 of the time). PWM dimming just shortens each LED's on-time further. No dimmer hardware is needed: it is the same timer in firmware. Brightness follows the AVERAGE LED current, and so does the energy: scanning 3 LEDs at 1.5 mA peak for 1/3 of the time looks the same and costs the same as 3 steady LEDs at 0.5 mA. Dimmer = lower duty = proportionally lower energy. (Free idea: the solar cell's own voltage reads ambient light, so firmware can auto-dim in dark rooms with no extra sensor. Not tested.)

## 5. What it means
- The always-on motion sensor is the biggest consumer, bigger than the 3-LED display: BMI270 wake mode (1.0 mWh/day by itself) vs a button (0). An accelerometer-only wake part (BMA400-class) cuts that ~15x.
- With a 3-LED dim display the whole watch is ~1.1-1.2 mWh/day (button or BMA400): a 100 mAh cell lasts ~10 months unaided; solar becomes a convenience, not a necessity.
- Small cell vs load (1.19 mWh/day, BMA400 + 60 wakes): KXOB25-07X1TF covers ~27% (office), 65% (commuter), >400% (outdoor). The big 23x25 cell covers ~200% / ~480% / ~3000%.
